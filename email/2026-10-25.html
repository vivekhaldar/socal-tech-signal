Editor’s pick 01

UCI Research Forum: The New Software Frontier ↗

Friday, October 30 · 1:00–5:00 p.m.; reception 5:00–6:00 p.m. · Irvine · Registration required · Campus parking $16

Researchers and industry practitioners compare emerging developer tools, AI-assisted development, and changes to software-engineering practice. Editor’s pick 02

OWASP LA: Building an Autonomous AI Pentester ↗

Wednesday, October 28 · 5:30–8:30 p.m. · Santa Monica · Free · Registration open

Bugcrowd's Ari Schorr and Hemang Thakkar examine the infrastructure separating a pentesting-agent demo from a system trusted with production access: runtime safety, kill switches, practitioner-validated skills, audit logging, and feedback from offensive-security specialists. Friday, October 30 · 11:00–11:50 a.m. · Riverside · Free · Student-focused colloquium

UCR: 3D Feature Maps for Robot Navigation and Manipulation ↗

UC San Diego’s Nikolay Atanasov presents explicit 3D maps that combine geometry, vision, and language for robot navigation and manipulation. The talk covers signed distance functions for motion planning and safety, then map tokens that extend the context of vision-language-action models. Meet in UC Riverside’s Student Success Center, Room 329. Choose it for a focused robotics research talk; visitors should confirm campus access before traveling.

UCI: Bayesian Feature Selection in High Dimensions ↗

Illinois statistics professor Feng Liang presents Variational Automatic Relevance Determination, a method that decides whether each feature contributes zero, linear, or nonlinear behavior to a sparse additive model. The talk includes coordinate-descent implementation and comparisons on simulated and real data. Choose it for statistical learning, interpretable feature selection, and efficient inference.

Choose it for cryptography, computer architecture, and the engineering path from research to trusted deployment.

Tuesday, November 3 · 4:00–5:00 p.m. PST · Irvine · Donald Bren Hall, Room 2011

UCI: Record Linkage for Streaming Data ↗

Colorado State’s Andee Kaplan presents a Bayesian approach to matching duplicate records across messy data sources as new files arrive. Recursive updates and a Markov chain Monte Carlo sampler aim to retain the inference quality of batch methods at much lower compute cost. Pick it for data engineering, entity resolution, and statistical methods for evolving datasets.
