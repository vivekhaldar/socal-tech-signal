Thursday, November 5 · 11:00 a.m.–Noon PST · Irvine · Donald Bren Hall, Room 4011 · Seminar series welcomes the public

UCI: Robust and Controllable Multimodal Learning ↗

Salman Asif examines how multimodal AI can keep working when inputs are missing and how to remove targeted concepts from learned models. The talk covers proxy tokens, Modality Self-Play, and targeted unlearning with SLUG. Choose it for a technical research session on making multimodal systems more adaptable and controllable rather than assuming every input will always be available.

Thursday, November 5 · 11:30 a.m.–1:00 p.m. PST · Santa Ana · Complimentary · Registration required

Cybersecurity Lunch and Learn with FBI Agent Alex Storino ↗

FBI supervisory special agent Alex Storino discusses current threats, prevention, incident response, and how AI is changing cybercrime, followed by questions. Greystone Technology hosts at 1851 East 1st Street, Suite 1220, with lunch included. The vendor-hosted program is centered on FBI education rather than a product demonstration; its RSVP form requires a company email, company name, and job title.

Thursday, November 5 · Noon–2:00 p.m. PST · Santa Ana · Free · RSVP open · Bring a laptop

AI Launch Lab: Build Your App with AI ↗

Harsh Mody and Jared Rummler lead a working session on scoping an MVP, setting up Claude Code or Codex, building, and troubleshooting. Bring an app idea or use the session to find a manageable starting point; beginners are welcome. Meet at Spoons Grill and Bar, 2601 Hotel Terrace. Choose it for time spent making something with help in the room, not just discussing AI tools.

Thursday, November 5 · 4:00–5:00 p.m. PST · Irvine · Donald Bren Hall, Room 6011

UCI: Learning-Assisted Inference in High Dimensions ↗

Zhenhua Lin presents max-sliced Wasserstein two-sample testing, using learned projections and neural witness functions to compare high-dimensional distributions. The research combines sample splitting with valid statistical inference while avoiding expensive repeated resampling. Pick this for statistical learning and evaluation methodology; it runs at the same time as the mechanism-design talk in Room 4011.

Thursday, November 5 · 4:00–5:00 p.m. PST · Irvine · Donald Bren Hall, Room 4011

UCI: Compensation and Budget-Feasible Mechanism Design ↗

Ioannis Anagnostides studies how to pay independent contributors while respecting a budget and encouraging truthful participation. The algorithms and game-theory talk covers incentive design and efficient approximation under subadditive valuations. Choose it for the mathematical foundations of marketplaces and distributed collaboration; it conflicts with the statistics seminar in Room 6011.

Friday, November 6 · 11:00 a.m.–Noon PST · Irvine · Donald Bren Hall, Room 6011

UCI: Security, Privacy, AI, and the Future of Naming ↗

Georgia Tech's Michael Bailey looks beyond DNS resolution to the security and governance of naming systems. The lecture covers encrypted DNS, naming abuse, measurement, privacy-versus-control tradeoffs, domain disputes, and AI-mediated discovery. Choose it for internet infrastructure and the ways naming decisions affect trust, security, and what users can find.

Friday, November 6 · 11:00 a.m.–Noon PST · Irvine · Interdisciplinary Science and Engineering Building, Room 1010

UCI: Designing Quantum Materials with Atomically Precise Films ↗

Qi Song examines low-dimensional quantum materials through atomically precise film synthesis. The program connects molecular-beam epitaxy, photoemission spectroscopy, and resonant X-ray scattering with antiferromagnetic nickelates and superconductivity. This is a specialist materials-engineering research talk, not a quantum-computing tutorial, and it overlaps with the DNS/security lecture.
