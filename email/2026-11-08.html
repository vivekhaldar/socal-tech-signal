UCI: Prediction, Confidence and Calibration in Deep Learning ↗

Padhraic Smyth examines uncertainty quantification and calibration in classifiers, large language models, and time-series foundation models. The talk connects confidence estimates with agents and human–AI interaction: when a model sounds certain, what evidence supports trusting it? UCI's Center for Machine Learning explicitly welcomes the public to its seminar series. Choose this for research on measuring uncertainty rather than treating fluent output as calibrated confidence.

Thursday, November 12 · 4:00–5:00 p.m. PST · Irvine · Donald Bren Hall 6011

UCI: Statistical Attribute Alignment for Black-Box Generative AI ↗

Princeton's Jason Klusowski studies how to align synthetic-data attributes when a generative model is available only through queries. The research uses batch sampling and examines asymptotically optimal model-query costs. This is a statistics and learning-theory session about controlling generated distributions without access to model internals, not a fine-tuning tutorial. Meet at UCI's Donald Bren Hall, Room 6011.

Friday, November 13 · 9:00–10:20 a.m. PST · Irvine · McDonnell Douglas Engineering Auditorium

UCI: 3D-Printed Ion Traps for Quantum Information Processing ↗

Berkeley's Hartmut Häffner presents micro ion traps made through two-photon-polymerization fabrication. The research covers calcium-ion confinement and coherent optical-qubit operations after Doppler cooling, connecting fabrication methods with scalable quantum information processing and precision measurement. Choose this for a hardware-oriented quantum research seminar. The physical meeting is at UCI's McDonnell Douglas Engineering Auditorium.

Friday, November 13 · 11:00 a.m.–Noon PST · Irvine · Calit2 Auditorium

UCI: Avi Wigderson on the Value of Errors in Proofs ↗

Avi Wigderson traces classical and modern definitions of proof, showing how interaction, randomness, and errors lead to surprising computational power. The journey connects Turing's work to the 2020 MIP*=RE breakthrough and its consequences for complexity theory, mathematics, and physics. UCI's announcement explicitly says no special mathematical background is required. Choose this for foundational computer science explained through the changing notion of a proof.

Tuesday, November 10 · 4:00–5:00 p.m. PST · Pasadena · Caltech, Annenberg 105

Caltech: The Quest for Representational Universals ↗

MIT's Phillip Isola examines common structures that emerge in learned representations. One result aligns vision and language embeddings up to rotation without paired data; another finds nearby solutions to downstream tasks in the weight neighborhoods of large models. The research asks whether increasingly large neural networks converge on simpler, more universal geometries. Choose this for a technical account of representation learning rather than an introduction to AI tools.
