Editor’s pick 01

Irvine AI: Self-Correcting Code Agents and X-Ray Models ↗

Thursday, November 19 · 5:30–7:30 p.m. PST; check-in from 5:00 · Irvine · Free · RSVP open

Team Artemis, PlanetDDS's AI-first innovation group, presents AI-assisted software development, agents that fix their own code, and a model that judges dental X-ray quality. Rahul Khot and Marcus Hobbs host at iQ Smart Center, 17872 Gillette, Suite 120. Technical sessions run 5:30–6:30, followed by food, beer, and conversation. Bring questions about actual engineering workflows; curious software engineers and technical leads are welcome.

Editor’s pick 02

LA DevOps: LLM Inference and Multi-Avatar Agents ↗

Thursday, November 19 · 6:00–9:00 p.m. PST · Santa Monica · Free · Registration open

Hrittik Roy digs into prefill/decode, KV caches, batching, GPU scheduling, and disaggregated inference with vLLM and NVIDIA Dynamo at 6:45 p.m. At 7:15, Arsen Yeremin demonstrates multi-avatar agents and Gaussian-splat characters running on a five-GPU Kubernetes cluster. Choose this for implementation details and live systems rather than agent slideware.

Editor’s pick 03

DEVx Paper Club: Weight-Space Programming ↗

Thursday, November 19 · 7:00–9:00 p.m. PST · San Diego · Free · Registration open

Matthew Love discusses MIT research on using LLMs to read, repair, and write neural-network weights directly, without gradient descent. The paper club welcomes questions and participants of all backgrounds; the focus is understanding the research, not a product demonstration. Meet at 11988 El Camino Real in the seminar space immediately off the lobby. Choose this for a technical discussion of what it would mean to program a model through its weights.

Wednesday, November 18 · 6:00–8:00 p.m. PST · Newport Beach · Free audience admission · Registration open

Ticket 500: Pitch Practice Night ↗

Practice the story, ask, and delivery of a founder pitch with practical audience feedback at Ticket 500, 2807 Villa Way. This is a low-stakes rehearsal, explicitly not an investor night. Wednesday, November 18 · 6:30–8:30 p.m. PST; talk at 7:00 · Irvine · Free · Group join-request approval required

OC ACM: The LLM Paradigm Shift ↗

Johnny Lin, Principal AI Scientist and Architect at Constellation Energy, traces the move from symbolic AI through word vectors and sequence models to Transformer attention, GPU scaling, instruction tuning, and tool execution. Meet at Knobbe Martens, 2040 Main Street, 14th floor. IEEE OC chapters co-sponsor the program. Parking-validation passes are distributed in the meeting room; request group membership in advance rather than treating the listing as an instant RSVP.

Thursday, November 19 · 11:00 a.m.–Noon PST · Irvine · Donald Bren Hall 4011 · Seminar series welcomes the public

UCI: Building Proactive Agents That Augment Human Work ↗

Stanford's Diyi Yang connects a workforce audit and analysis of nearly 250,000 human–AI conversations with proactive assistance. General User Models infer knowledge, preferences, and context from computer interactions, while Next Action Prediction reasons over multimodal histories. UCI's Center for Machine Learning explicitly welcomes the public to its seminars. Choose this for research on when and how agents should intervene in human work.

Thursday, November 19 · 2:00–3:00 p.m. PST · Irvine · McDonnell Douglas Engineering Auditorium

UCI: Polymer Microstructure and Nucleic-Acid Delivery ↗

Explore how gradient-copolymer microstructure affects binding, stability, delivery, and editing efficiency for plasmid DNA and CRISPR/Cas9 ribonucleoproteins. A second portion examines asymmetric flow field-flow fractionation as an analytical and preparative technique. This specialist materials and bioengineering seminar links molecular design to delivery performance. Meet at UCI's McDonnell Douglas Engineering Auditorium.

Thursday, November 19 · 4:00–5:00 p.m. PST · Irvine · Donald Bren Hall 6011

UCI: Rating Games with Strength-Dependent Tie Probabilities ↗

Harvard statistician Mark Glickman models a draw as a third outcome whose probability depends on competitor strength, rather than treating it as half a win and half a loss. The talk covers Bayesian dynamic models, approximate Newton–Raphson posterior updates, and evaluation on correspondence-chess data. Choose this for statistical ideas relevant to rankings, matchmaking, and game systems. Meet in UCI's Donald Bren Hall 6011.

Friday, November 20 · 11:00 a.m.–Noon PST · Irvine · Donald Bren Hall 6011

UCI: LLM-Based Auto-Formalization for Trustworthy Planning ↗

Drexel's Li “Harry” Zhang uses LLMs to formalize environments and goals into languages such as PDDL, then delegates planning to deterministic solvers. The research examines evaluation under constraints, inference-time methods, and iterative or multi-agent planning with partial observability. Meet in UCI's Donald Bren Hall 6011. Choose this for a precise division of responsibilities between language models and formal planning systems.

Friday, November 20 · 11:00 a.m.–Noon PST · Irvine · Interdisciplinary Science and Engineering Building 1010

UCI: Materials and Models for Biopurification ↗

Nick Vecchiarello of the University of Virginia connects peptide-based mixed-mode chromatography resins, high-throughput screening, and mechanistic models of thermodynamics, kinetics, and transport. The goal is adaptable purification workflows and in-silico process simulation for emerging biologics. This is a specialist biomanufacturing alternative to the simultaneous CS planning talk, not a two-stop itinerary. Meet in UCI ISEB 1010.

Friday, November 20 · 1:00–2:00 p.m. PST · Irvine · Donald Bren Hall 6011 · In-person option

UCI: The DARPA Tutor and Teaching Complex Skills ↗

Richard Burton describes how expert one-to-one teaching was encoded into a sixteen-week Navy IT and networking tutor. The session covers program design and evaluation; the organizer reports outcomes comparable to personnel with ten years of experience. Choose this for the construction and assessment of systems that teach complex skills. The physical session meets in UCI's Donald Bren Hall 6011; a Zoom option is separate.

Caltech: Material Platforms for Neuromorphic Computing ↗

Alex Frano explores how trainable material responses can be co-designed with algorithms, connecting hydrogenated nickelates and vanadium dioxide with neuromorphic computing. The question is not merely how to mimic a neuron in software, but how a physical system's behavior can participate in computation. Refreshments begin at 4:00 p.m.; the research seminar meets in Noyes 147.

OWASP LA: Why Incident Response Plans Fail Under Pressure ↗

Cassio Goldschmidt examines why an incident-response plan that reads well can fail during a real emergency. The session covers cognitive offloading, pre-authorized responses, role clarity, realistic simulations, and blameless postmortems. Meet at 929 Colorado Avenue. Choose this for concrete ways to reduce the number of decisions responders must improvise under pressure, rather than another checklist of security products.

UCLA: AI That Understands Space and Motion ↗

Achuta Kadambi examines how to lift two-dimensional foundation models into queryable, editable three-dimensional scenes and four-dimensional video.
